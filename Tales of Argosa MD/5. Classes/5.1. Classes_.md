ToA has nine classes to choose from: **Artificer, Barbarian, Bard, Cultist, Fighter, Magic User, Monk, Ranger,** and **Rogue**. Not all classes will suit all games (particularly Artificer, at least for a sword & sorcery setting), so check with your GM to confirm which are available.

### Hit Points

While hit points partly reflect the ability to absorb blows, they are primarily more **intangible** qualities such as fighting spirit, combat experience, stamina, and luck. Being restored to full hit points after a battle does not mean a PC's wounds miraculously heal. On the contrary, it suggests that despite their injuries, the adventurer has gathered the courage to rally, found their second wind, or is otherwise hell bent on reaching their objective.

All 1st level PCs start with **hit points equal to their Con score plus class hp bonus** (generally 1-3 points). Between 2nd and 9th level, all characters increase their hp by their class hp bonus only.

> [!example]
> A 1st level Con 13 Fighter starts play with 16 hit points (Con 13 + 3 class hp bonus). At 2nd level her hp increases by 3 to 19, then to 22 at 3rd level, etc.

### Wounded

Most damage is cuts and scrapes, but if reduced to **half max hp** (known as **Wounded**), the character shows real signs of trauma. If reduced to zero hp, the subject is unconscious or incapacitated, and may die or suffer a persistent injury (see [[13.1. Injury & Death_|p.83]]).

### Levelling Up

When a PC levels up they gain **+1 to an attribute** of their choice (excluding *Luck* and Init, up to max 16), more **hit points**, and other benefits as outlined in the **Class Attack Bonus & Abilities** tables. If a spell or monster ability refers to a target's [[21.1. Monsters_#Hit Dice|HD]], a PC's HD equals their level.

### Class Abilities

All classes come with special abilities relating to their general theme or niche. Some abilities work automatically while others require a check of some kind. PCs may attempt to recover expended abilities by taking **[[14.1. Short Rest_|Short]]** or **[[14.3. Long Rest_|Long Rests]]**.

### Unique Features

**Every 3rd level** devise one new ability, trait, or theme for your adventurer. Feel free to borrow feats, class abilities, perks and so on from other games, modified to suit ToA. The advancement need not be limited to traditional class themes, allowing for a degree of multi-classing. The **open nature** of Unique Features requires some table discussion to keep things balanced and consistent with the genre of your campaign. Consider these advancements under constant playtesting and subject to **tweaking**.

If uncomfortable with creating your own Unique Features, **some examples** appear on [[7.1. Unique Features_|p.45]]. For more experienced players, GMs might allow a bonus Unique Feature at 1st level to allow for greater PC customisation. Tables favouring simplicity might ignore UFs altogether (or grant +1 to an Attribute, max 16).

> [!note] Designer's Thoughts
> Hit points in *ToA* start out a little higher than LFG, but increase at a slower rate. The flatter hp curve has many benefits: PCs quickly reach and remain in the hp "sweet spot" of 20-30, lower HD monsters remain dangerous for longer, fewer enemies are needed to threaten the party, and most importantly; fights are quicker and more exciting! This in turn frees up precious session time for more exploration, social interactions, side treks, crazy plans, and of course - more fighting (and looting)!
