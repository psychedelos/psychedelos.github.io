Ritual magic allows a caster to impose a long term magical effect on an object, area, or creature(s), including for example a staff, building, or a pile of corpses. The duration of the enchantment may be months, years, or even permanent at the GM's discretion. Especially potent effects might require multiple spell casters working together.

A ritual requires the following:

- At least 2d6 days to perform (GM's call).
- A successful (i) spell casting check, and (ii) *Luck* (Will) save, from each caster.
- Each caster must pay a price (eg 1 point of Con or *Luck* loss, 3d6 years aging, etc; GM's call), which cannot be recovered as long as the effect persists (if not permanent).

Boundaries of area effects must be marked in some way (runes, tokens, etc), and are generally limited to a Close or Far sized area.

### Components

Every ritual requires expensive components (GM determines, but a minimum of **3d6 x 100 sp**) and a **special reagent** that the PC must provide. The GM decides the component based on the **theme** of the effect, or **rolls d66** on the table below. Ritual magic is more mercurial art than science; the time and special component needed may change each time a ritual is attempted.

### Examples

Examples might include enchanting a talisman, calling a familiar, conjuring an elemental, binding a pair of shades to guard a tomb, setting a lightning rune trap, creating a persistent illusion, etc. Effects, duration, and other conditions are GM's call.
