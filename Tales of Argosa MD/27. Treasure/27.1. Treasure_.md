When the party starts looting the bodies of the fallen, it's time to roll for treasure! Some foes might not carry anything (animals, giant vermin, etc), but most intelligent humanoids will have something. Treasure is split into the following categories:

- **Carry Loot A** Often nothing of significance, or a small number of copper or silver coins. Low chance of a precious stone.
- **Carry Loot B** Mostly silver coins or a useful item. Possibly some gold coins. Low chance of a gemstone.
- **Valuables A** Lesser art objects, lesser gems, potions, scrolls. Low chance of a Minor Charm.
- **Valuables B** Greater art objects, greater gems, potions, scrolls. Low chance of a Minor Charm or Major Magical Item.
- **Trinkets & Curios** Broad variety of weird and wonderful curiosities.
- **Lair Treasure** Major hauls.
- **Minor Magical Items** Potions, scrolls, spellbooks, and Minor Charms.
- **Major Magical Items** Higher power permanent magic items.

GMs are encouraged to customise the treasure tables to suit their campaign and/or the scenario at hand.

> [!note] Carry Loot A
> Carry Loot generally reflects the kinds of things creatures might have on their person, such as small pouches of coins and pocketable portable items like antitoxin, smelling salts, candles, etc.
>
> *[[27.1.1. Carry Loot A|Carry Loot A]]* is the lowest value treasure chart, and a single **d100** roll generally represents the total spoils of a small group of enemies. Most rolls will generate 0 - 50 sp, with some exceptions up to 120 sp or so (lesser precious stone).

![[27.1.1. Carry Loot A]]

### Carry Loot B

*[[27.1.2. Carry Loot B|Carry Loot B]]* tends to include larger silver amounts, useful mundane items, and limited gold. A single **d100** roll generally represents the total spoils of a small group. Most rolls will generate 60 - 200 sp, or fewer coins coupled with a useful item. There is a small chance of a gemstone worth 400 - 1,300 sp.
