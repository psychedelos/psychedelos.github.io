Using adventures from other systems with Tales of Argosa is fairly painless. Some tips appear below.

### Monsters

Generally speaking the easiest way to convert **Monsters** is to simply **swap out** the adventure's creatures for ToA equivalents.

- If there's no precise **Monster** match, choose a similar creature and tweak to taste.
- **B/X, OSE, Shadowdark, AD&D** and similar old school monsters can be ported across pretty much as is. **Based on HD**, consult the **[[21.2. Custom Monsters_|Custom Monsters]]** table to determine **Luck,** and compare **AC** & **Dmg**. Swapping multiple attacks for a single higher damage attack is preferable. Add a **Nat 19** effect; the default is a *[[13.2. Trauma Tables_|Trauma]]* roll, but it can be anything that fits the monster's theme. If the original stat block does not include **Str, Dex, etc**, improvise as required (these scores are often not needed outside of Contests) or use a similar monster's stats.
- **5e** monsters tend to have larger hp pools than ToA uses. Substitute **HD** based on a similar Argosa monster, and apply **AC** and **Damage** expressions using the **[[21.2. Custom Monsters_|Custom Monster]]** table. Add a **Nat 19** effect; the default is a *[[13.2. Trauma Tables_|Trauma]]* roll, but special monster abilities also make good candidates. **Str, Dex, etc** scores will generally carry across okay. If Legendary Actions apply, use **[[21.3. Special Traits_#Boss|Boss]]** or **[[21.3. Special Traits_#Heavy|Heavy]]** templates.

### NPCs

For classed NPCs use **[[24.1. Rival Adventurers_|Rival Adventurers]]**. For non-classed NPCs, use the Human or similar stat blocks.

### Spells & Traps

- If there's no equivalent **Spell**, improvise from the existing spell list, use a potion or magic item effect, or treat the effect as Ritual Magic.
- If there's no equivalent **Trap**, improvise from the traps list or apply a *[[13.2. Trauma Tables_|Trauma]]* roll.

### Treasure

Some adventures have much larger amounts of treasure than ToA assumes. The easiest way to convert loot is to **swap out** all treasure in the adventure for Argosa equivalents:

- Use **[[27.1. Treasure_|Carry Loot A & B]]** rolls for small groups.
- **[[27.2. Valuables_|Valuables A & B]]** rolls for more precious finds.
- **[[27.3.1. Trinkets & Curios|Trinkets & Curios]]** for oddball or unusual items.
- **[[27.4.1. Lair Treasure|Lair Treasure]]** rolls are generally reserved for the consolidated hordes of very large numbers of creatures (eg scores or hundreds) as opposed to, for example, a "lair" of twelve *[[Ogre|Ogres]]* (which might instead consist of a [[27.1.2. Carry Loot B|Carry Loot B]] and [[27.2.1. Valuables A|Valuables A]] roll).

If you prefer to stick with the original adventure coin amounts, **swap out** platinum coins for gold, **gold for silver**, and silver for copper. Ignore any original copper amounts. For **extremely large hordes**, eg thousands of coins, replace them with a **[[27.4.1. Lair Treasure|Lair Treasure]]** roll instead.

Similarly, some adventures have prolific numbers of **Magical Items** (one shot or permanent). It is not necessarily a problem to use more magical items, but the party will be stronger than usual, and DDM will trigger more often. If you prefer to maintain a low to moderate magic game:

- Where an adventure has multiple enchanted items in a single location, **ignore all but one,** swapping it for an equivalent ToA item, or roll on the **[[27.5.1. Minor Magical Items|Minor]], [[27.8.1. Major Magical Items|Major]],** or **Magic Item Generator** tables.
- For **Magical Weapons** and **Armour**, a great alternative is to swap in a **Special Metal** or **[[9.15. Masterforged Gear_|Masterforged]]** item instead.
