Published adventures are great for solo. You can kick off with zero prep and you get to play with all the cool stuff someone else made! Sweet maps, amazing illustrations, cards, dice, tokens, minis - you name it! Make up your PC(s), add some background music, and you're good to go.

Generally speaking running the adventure unfolds much like group play, but you are both **GM and player,** switching back and forth between what your PC(s) are doing and how the world responds.

My preferred approach is to **only read as much of the adventure as you need to**, based on interactions with your party and no more. Do not read the GM overview! Just find the hook, figure out where the adventure site is, and decide how your PCs are going to get there (or just kick off in media res at the site, or in the starting situation).

Some **layouts** are **more solo friendly** than others. I find a separation between "what you can see" and "what happens if you interact with it" makes things easier (for example, room intro above with bullet points below), and random tables within the adventure itself keeps the surprises coming.

Some consider that using published material will inevitably lead to **"spoilers"**, and that's true from a player only perspective. But in solo you are also the GM; if you play your adventurer based on their knowledge, "spoilers" aren't a problem. If you're genuinely unsure what your PCs would do, make an **attribute check** or ***Consult the Bones.***

After you're done with the adventure, you might like to **go back and read it** to find out what you missed. Sometimes what the author intended and the adventure you get are two very different things! I like to think of published material as a series of possibilities; what actually unfolds at the table is what's important.
