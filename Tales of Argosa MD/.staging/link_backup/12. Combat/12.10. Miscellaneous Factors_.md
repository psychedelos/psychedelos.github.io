### Cover

A character half covered by terrain gains a +2 bonus to AC and *Luck* (Dex) saves, or +4 if three quarters covered. A character fully covered by terrain cannot be directly attacked unless the cover is somehow bypassed.

### Visibility

A target that is **very hard to see**, glimpsed in near darkness, or moving in and out of heavy fog or similar, is protected by a **33% miss** chance against any direct attack (*Near Blind*).

A character who **cannot be seen** at all due to total darkness, impenetrable fog, invisibility or similar, is protected by a **66% miss chance** on all direct attacks, assuming an attacker can narrow down his location by some means (*Blind*). If the attacker cannot narrow down the location, no effective direct attack may be attempted. Area effect attacks may not be subject to this miss chance in certain situations, at the GM's discretion.

> [!example]
> A sorcerer the party is fighting turns invisible. *Kruncor* notices tell tale footprints in the long grass, and hurls his axe! The adventurer makes an attack roll as normal, and succeeds. The GM then rolls for the 66% invisibility miss chance, scoring 77. It's a hit! The axe lodges itself in the sorcerer's skull with a wet thud, dropping his reappearing form to the ground as *Kruncor* roars victorious!

### Invisible or Stealthy Attacker

An **invisible** attacker gains advantage on attacks. A **stealthy** (but not invisible) attacker who sneaks up on or shoots at an unaware enemy (eg suddenly moving out from behind a wall to fire), might gain the benefits of surprise (GM's call).

### Surprise

An attacker who gets the drop on an unaware opponent gains a **bonus round** (see Initiative p.69). Further, the ambusher's **first attack** is made at advantage, or **physical area effect** imposes disad on any *Luck* save.

### Non-Proficiency

The GM decides whether a character can use a weapon that is not on their class weapon list. If yes, apply **disadvantage** to their attack rolls, or some other penalty as the referee determines.

### Knock Outs

If using a weapon to knock out, rather than kill, an opponent, the character suffers a **-2 penalty** to attack, and if the hit reduces the target to zero hp, the target is rendered senseless but remains alive. Such a prisoner generally regains consciousness with 1 hit point after **2d6 minutes.** Unarmed knock out attempts do not suffer the -2 penalty to attack.

### Underwater Combat

Adventurers have an unfortunate habit of getting into underwater struggles with monsters trying to drown them. When fighting in an underwater melee, piercing weapons operate normally, slashing weapons attack at disad, and clubbing weapons are useless. Arrows moving through water only inflict damage up to about 10 ft deep.

### Flanking

*ToA* uses abstract positioning consistent with movement range bands, as reflected in the **Outnumber Enemies** modifier entry in the Melee Modifiers Table on p.77. If however your table is using precise positioning, allies gain a +1 attack bonus when flanking an enemy (ie with an ally on the opposite side).

### Drowning and Suffocation

Adventurers laden with equipment can normally hold their breath and swim (or perform other activity) for approximately 1 round per point of Con, plus another 1d4 rounds. While fighting however, the base duration is a number of rounds equal to one third Con score (round up). After this period, the adventurer must make a *Luck* (Con) save at the end of each round or be reduced to zero hp. The GM decides whether the PC might be able to be resuscitated if rescued quickly.

### Aerial Combat

An aerial combatant gains the following:

- +1 bonus on attack rolls against ground based targets, and may make attacks part way through their move (in a single direction).
- Gains the Rogue *Skirmisher* ability, and may make a special charge attack causing double damage if the target is surprised.

### Other Modifiers

A 1 or 2 point modifier will cover most nebulous combat factors, eg high ground, slippery surfaces, etc. GMs should use as many or as few modifiers as they like. The referee decides if modifiers stack.
