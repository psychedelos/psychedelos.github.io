Running a session of ToA is fairly **easy**, firstly because there aren't many rules, and secondly because the **GM is encouraged to change the rules** however they please. Everything in this document is just a guide, and referees are **expected to tweak** things to their liking, in consultation with their players. Ultimately the GM is the final authority for his or her game.

As far as GM advice goes, there are plenty of excellent free game blogs on the internet for those who are interested. In ToA, most situations can be handled by the referee **improvising rulings** based on the situation at hand, and whether a **check** of some kind is required, using the existing rules as a guide. **Attribute checks** and an **eyeballed modifier** will handle most situations.

> [!note]
> If the party attempts to sneak up on a group of sleeping goblins, is a Dex check required, or do the players automatically succeed?
>
> There is **no right or wrong answer** to this question, and **perfectly reasonable GMs will differ** in their approaches. Some referees might allow the PCs to automatically sneak up on their foes. Others will call for a Dex check, perhaps with advantage. Others might *Consult the Bones* or decide a simple 10% chance one of the goblins randomly wakes. All four approaches are reasonable, and as long as the GM tends to make **consistent rulings for similar situations**, the players will know what to expect and all will be well.
>
> There is one piece of advice I will offer: **embrace emergent play!** The most fun adventures often arise from improvised events, and ToA has all the tools required: theatre of the mind friendly, tables galore, oracles, and procedures for exploring land, air, and sea. Argosa's rules are well suited to a **sandbox** approach, where the **game world is independent** of the adventurers and does not "scale" to provide "level appropriate" or "balanced" encounters. Party Retreat, Chases, Reaction rolls, and Morale allow you to **throw whatever makes sense at the party,** safe in the knowledge that a TPK is relatively unlikely. If the adventurers get in over their heads, they have options to (and probably will) escape.

Whether out of the blue, or a spin off from a GM hook, if the **players latch onto something** and push the session in a specific direction - go with it! Don't resist and try to "steer" them back to the original adventure idea. Instead put on your improv cap, ready your oracles, fire up that imagination, and **enjoy the ride!**

The **freedom to explore and try anything** is the key strength of tabletop RPGs. The referee and players are more interactive and flexible than any computer game, Netflix series, or written story. Flex that imagination and make gaming history!
