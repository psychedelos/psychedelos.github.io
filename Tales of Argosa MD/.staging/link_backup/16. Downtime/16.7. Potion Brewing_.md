Magic Users and Cultists may brew potions during Downtime, and at the GM's option, appropriately trained apothecaries, alchemists, and Artificers. A PC requires access to a **laboratory** or apparatus friendly workroom (**1,000 sp**) for the process.

Brewing a potion **costs (1d4+2) x 100 sp** and is not without risk. More art than science, the process requires **2d6 days** of constant attention, and a successful **Int (Apothecary\* or Arcane Lore\*)** check. Any potion from the Potion List (p.239) may be created, as long as the brewer has the required special ingredient. On a **success** the potion is made, and the creator is **drained 1 Con.** Lost Con cannot be restored until the end of the next Downtime period after the potion has been consumed.

On a **failure** the brewing fails and any materials are lost (or, GM's call, the potion is made but when used also inflicts 1d12 *Toxin Trauma*). On a Terrible Failure, a catastrophic explosion occurs, destroying the laboratory and reducing the brewer to zero hp.

### Special Ingredient

Every magic potion requires a **special ingredient** that the PC must supply (generally some part of the anatomy of a monstrous creature, which may need to be preserved). The following table provides suggestions, but as always GMs should tweak as desired. Monsters with qualities that are somehow consistent with the potion theme are most appropriate.

At the GM's option, "greater monster" parts might substitute or boost lesser monster ingredients. For example, using *Dragon* tongue in a Dragon Breath potion (as opposed to *Hell Hound* lungs) might cause 10d10 damage instead of 10d6.
