This chapter provides a selection of Downtime activities for PCs to **spend their silver** on, to keep them hungry for coin. As a result, most of what follows is meant to **extract wealth from the party**, not the reverse. **Prices** are based on average treasure tables, and ballpark Adventure Framework loot rewards. Referees should adjust costs to suit their setting and wealth levels.

### Downtime Period

In a sandbox game, the **players generally decide** how long their downtime is between adventures, based on whatever it is they want to achieve: injury recovery, magical research, rumour hunting, waiting for a masterforged item, and so on. As the weeks tick by, PCs should be paying their **Lifestyle** costs in addition to any extra activities.

While this is going on, other **factions** in the setting are probably continuing to pursue their goals, etc, which may impact the party later. If the GM would like fate to determine **how much time passes** before **something interesting** happens, **roll 2d6**:

The average result of 2d4 weeks to two months is intended to cover the typical injury recovery period for a broken bone or similar persistent injury.

To **find out what** the event is, **roll 1d6**:

**Enemy Action** means an opposing faction takes a major step or completes its plans. **Forced Scenario** is intended as an adventure that comes to the PCs and forces a response: an assassination attempt on the party, a relative is kidnapped, the King commands they undertake a quest, and so on.

> [!note] Buildings
> For adventurers who wish to buy or build their own home, tower, small keep, etc, rough costs appear below. **Construction times** are **4d6 days** for **small**, **2d4 weeks** for **medium**, and **2d6 months** for **large**. **Greater** structures take **multiple years**.
