During Downtime, adventurers with **Addictions**, **Madness**, or **Persistent Injuries** must spend some time dealing with them.

### Addiction

A character with an Addiction is subject to powerful cravings. Players might like to roleplay their Addiction for [[16.2. Advancement_#Social|social xp]], and **once per adventure** (GM's call, typically during high stress), the GM may require the PC to make a ***Luck*** (**Will) save**. On a fail, if the PC does not get their fix within one hour, they suffer ***[[13.2.5. Toxin Trauma|Toxin Trauma]]*** (1d8, lasts 1d6 hours) and **1 Con loss**. Lost Con cannot be recovered until the Addiction is removed.

During **Downtime**, a PC with an Addiction must make a **Will** check. On a success, they may choose to either (i) feed their habit, or (ii) spend time trying to break it. If they fail the Will check, they must feed their Addiction.

- **Feeding an Addiction** costs **10 sp per week** (Rich or Filthy Rich [[9.8. Lifestyle_|Lifestyles]] include this cost). At the GM's option, an adventurer with a long standing Addiction may find it begins to affect NPC relationships, and/or the per week cost increases. If the PC cannot afford to get their fix for the week, they suffer *[[13.2.5. Toxin Trauma|Toxin Trauma]]* and Con loss as noted earlier.
- **Breaking the Habit** requires a minimum Poor Lifestyle and **eight weeks** of sequestered focus, precluding other Downtime activities. On a ***Luck*** **(Will) save,** the Addiction goes into remission and is removed, but future risks of Addiction increase by 10%. The PC may spend **150 sp** for **treatment** (counselling, herbalism, etc) to gain a +2 bonus on the save. On a **failure** the time is lost and the PC remains addicted. On a **Terrible Failure**, the per week cost increases by 10 sp, and at the GM's option cravings may occur more than once per adventure.

### Madness Remission

Every **eight weeks** a PC makes a ***Luck*** (**Will) save** for each Madness they have. A **success** means the Madness goes into remission and is removed. On a **failure** the Madness worsens such that no further recovery attempts may be attempted until the PC gains a level. Additionally, on a **Terrible Failure**, the Madness triggers more than once per adventure, as determined by the GM ([[19.1. Madness_#Acute Episodes|p.160]]). Two month's **counselling** from a trained carer (at a cost of **150 sp**) grants a +2 bonus on the save.

### Persistent Injuries

Some persistent injuries such as broken limbs require treatment and time to heal (as detailed in the injury tables). **Nursing** care costs **35 sp/week**, and **apothecaries 70 sp/week.** Rich or Filthy Rich [[9.8. Lifestyle_|Lifestyles]] include this cost).

### Permanent Injuries

GM's might permit adventurers with permanent injuries, such as lost eyes or limbs, to **reduce or negate** some or all related **penalties** after **three months** of adaption training. For example, a fighter with a missing eye might negate the 33% miss chance after acclimatizing to one eye, or a rogue with a peg leg reduce her movement penalties. The GM decides these on a case by case basis.

> [!note] Designer's Thoughts
> Persistent injuries are intended to make the game more dangerous with longer term setbacks that are not easily overcome (unlike lost hp or expended class abilities). Even permanent injuries however are not truly permanent; PCs are expected to adapt as noted above. **Starting characters** that happen to roll missing ears etc (using the Hireling tables) are assumed to have already adapted to their injuries.
